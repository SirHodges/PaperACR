Filling out the ACR

What do I do if I run out of space on the ACR?

Start a second ACR. Because it's a continuation rather than a new patient, strike through the front page of the second form (Clinical Info / Physical Exam) with a single line and initial it. Then:

  • The Call # must be on every page.
  • Number the pages correctly (1/2, 2/2, and so on).
  • If the call involves more than one patient, complete the demographic section on the additional form too.

This applies whether you've run out of room in the procedures section or in the incident history / remarks (for example, on a code 72).

Do I have to enter the call number on the second page?

Yes — the Call # must be recorded on every page of the ACR.

Can I enter "Transfer" or "MVA (MVC)" in the Chief Complaint area?

No. The Chief Complaint should reflect the patient's expressed issue — in the patient's own words whenever possible. A mechanism of injury (like an MVC) isn't a complaint, so it doesn't belong in this section.

How do I know which code goes in Primary Problem vs. Secondary Problem?

Choose the codes that best represent the patient's primary issue. They're listed on the back of the ACR, and the complete list of ministry problem codes is available on ontario.ca.

For the call type, do I check every box that applies or just the most pertinent one?

Consult the completion reference on the Form guide for how this section should be filled out.

Will there be a "Mandatory Fields" guide?

All fields on the paper form are mandatory — there should be no blank fields once the ACR is complete. For anything that genuinely can't be filled in, use CNO or an initialed single-line strikethrough rather than leaving it blank.

How do the patient and sequence fields work on multi-patient or multi-vehicle calls?

Coordinate with the other responders on scene to assign patient sequence numbers, so everyone's ACRs line up. On complex MVRs with multiple patients, the Ops Supt on scene should help sort this out.

On the call — times, dispatch & data

When can we call Comms for times?

Please don't call Comms for times. You can get your Call # and times from MCAD — connect with your Supt if you need help with that process.

Exception: you'll need to call CACC for times if you ran a call outside the Ottawa CACC borders.

Do we need to call dispatch with VSA information?

Yes. They need to know:

  • Whether it's suspicious or not
  • The patient's name
  • Code 5 vs. code 6
  • The BHP name/number if it's a pronouncement
What priority do I document if I was upgraded or downgraded en route?

Document the priority you were on when you booked 10-7. If anything about it is unusual — say you were downgraded but the patient turned out to be CTAS 1/2 on assessment — add a note in remarks or file an IR.

Should I be transmitting my defib data?

Yes, please — keep uploading it every time. The data uploads to Stryker, who hold it for us for up to 30 days.

Is there a hospital code for Gatineau?

Hospital number codes are for Ontario hospitals only. Use QGAT or QHUL for Gatineau or Hull. For any other Quebec hospital, call dispatch for the answer.

Paperwork & process

Which envelope should PRUs and special teams use?

All regular Ops T3s should use the new black-and-white envelopes. Spec Ops and PRU are fine to use up the old coloured (purple/orange) envelopes, as long as they fill out:

  • Date, names, Call #, times
  • Patient name, pickup location, transport destination
  • Truck number and DEFIB
What do I do about an amendment if the ACR and shift pack are already submitted to QA?

Email ParamedicQualityReview@Ottawa.ca and your Supt with the reason you need the form back.

Do we still complete an ACR for every vehicle on scene, or move to one document per patient?

For now, please continue to complete an ACR for each vehicle that 10-7s. It goes a long way to help the records techs reconcile a large volume of paperwork while they smooth out their filing process. We'll revisit this as things settle.
